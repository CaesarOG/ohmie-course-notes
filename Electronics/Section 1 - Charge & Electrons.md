# Resistors
Resistors have Ohms which specify how much they squeeze/slow down electron flow (current). They literally resist flow of current as in the name. They keep current in check. If you have an LED after the resistor and nothing else in the circuit, the resistor decides how much current the LED gets, too little resistance and it will burn out, too much is okay technically but current will trickle past and LED will be very dim.

# Current (Electricity)
Electrons move through a metal wire, that is the flow of current. When there is a voltage (potential difference) and a closed circuit, current flows. When there is a current, the electrons rather than just jitter around, drift downwards of potential difference. So charge (electrons) being present $\neq$ charge flowing, must drift together in organized motion for current. So then electric current is: charge in organized motion.

So say an LED not glowing, has wire feeding it already full of tiny charges. They look parked but up close jiggle non stop, just lack shared direction. With direction, drift together. Moving-together charge IS electricity. Take the shared direction away (potential difference) and it’s gone. Charges stay jiggling stays, only drift stops. Electricity isn’t the stuff (electrons) that fills the wire, it’s an activity the charges do, the drifting together. The jiggling charges, drifting as one.

A battery may be full of charge, but electricity is only where charge is drifting together. Battery charge only jiggling/waiting.

# Inside the Wire
Outer layer is soft outer jacket, under it, a woven shield hugging everything inside and keeping signal honest. Next, under the shield, each strand wrapped in its own plastic wall. Within those plastic walls each strand is bare shiny copper. This is where charges live and where marching of current.

Jiggling never stops current or no current. Resting wire isn’t short of movement agreement, no shared/together direction so no current. Copper being a conductor, lets them drift together when there is a potential difference.

# Electrons
Every atom of every wire has them, they do the organized motion the drifting together. Obviously everything is made of atoms, and every atom carries these tiny charges, but the inner ones are gripped tight, atom never lets them go. Valence electrons are jiggling around, and in a conductor like a metal, they barely hang on, loitering at every edge of its atom one nudge from freedom. So conductors therefore allow electrons to drift/march together when a potential difference is applied.

When one jumps off, it goes off into the gaps between atoms, atom it left keeps its place in the lattice, now one charge short. It’s not alone. Copper is a SEA of them, billions of billions of loose electrons, jiggling every which way, no agreement of direction. Then when a potential difference applied, all drift together down hill, and of course that organized drift is current.

While wire responds at once, all charges were already everywhere, waiting ready to move. A battery only applies potential difference that pushes the charges already there, it never supplies the charges. It pushes existing charges, doesn’t supply or add any. The conductive wire supplies the charges that will move. Pipes come pre filled, pumps only push. So once current flows, the valence electrons ie. loosely held outer ones, move through. Atoms stay parked in their lattice.

From metal to current:

1. A metal holds a sea of mobile electrons
2. Their jiggling has no net direction
3. A push arrives across a complete path
4. Whole sea drifts down hill together
5. Electric current is flowing

Sea, jiggle, push drift, current. Instant the push arrives, whole wire drifts together at once, no waiting for one electron to travel the distance.

Before any battery arrives, wire already full of moveable charge. Full - metal atoms’ outer valence electrons wander, so every wire is full of these before any potential difference from battery. These mobile electrons are the movers that carry current. If wires started empty you’d wait for charge to travel whole cable, instead push organizes movers which were already everywhere at once, that’s why light bulb answers instantly.

# Conductors & Insulators
Every material is either:

- Conductor (many electrons set free, ie. 1-3 valence in outer orbital so looks to give up)
- Insulator (held tight, outer shell full or almost, so looks to gain)

Highways and walls, safe circuit needs both.

For a conductor like copper, when no potential difference active, atoms stay put free electrons jiggling and ready. Once voltage active, atoms stay put but free electrons start drifting together. Outer shell few valence electrons, set free as atoms want to give for stability. So the conductor is crowded with free movers, jiggling between atoms. Voltage and they all drift together downhill instantly. Free movers and potential difference push, that is conducting. They allow charge to flow. Conductors include metals, salt water.

For an inductor like rubber, atoms bind every electron tightly and not free to jump as the outer orbital is full or near full. Plenty of electrons, and none of them set free as atoms want to gain for stability. Voltage and almost nothing, charges shift a whisker in place straining against their atoms, but stay put. No free movers no flow, that is insulating. Insulators include rubber, wood, glass, ceramic, plastic.

Same voltage potential difference, but conductor valence electrons flow/drift, insulator ones stay held in place.

One sneaky exception, salt water conducts not with loose valence electrons, but with charged ions, swimming. And you are mostly salt water.

Static Electricity: charge gets separated during vigorous physical contact, comes off of one surface onto another. Rubbing certain materials together can transfer elecrrons form one surface to the other. So electrons come off one go onto the other, then one is positive other is negative. Usually conducting surface ends up giving charge to be positive, insulating surface carries the negative charge electrons. This leaves potential that they will jump back somewhere to equalize, and that jump creates momentary electricity during the movement. The spark is the charge escaping all at once, charge in a hurry. Example balloon collects/hoards the extras, imbalance attracts hair/paper etc. balloon pulls and tugs, but insulator holds tight so hard to jump. A wall or paper is neutral but charged surface shuffles charge inside it polarization, near face turns opposite and opposite attracts. A conductor like you will spark when near metal to equalize. Get close enough to metal which is conductor and extras jump to equalize for balance, creating spark. Lightning is the same jump.
